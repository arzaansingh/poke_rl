\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

\guideblock{Paragraph 1 --- motivation. Tabular RL ceiling effect in
stochastic, partially-observable two-player games. Pokemon as a
$10^{12}$-state benchmark. Why the single-agent ceiling is the
interesting problem. One concrete sentence on Pokemon's combination of
move stochasticity, hidden opponent team, and turn-by-turn switch
options. Cite \citet{simoes2020}, \citet{sahovic2020pokeenv} for the
benchmark.}

\guideblock{Paragraph 2 --- why ensembling at all. Brief setup of the
question: in supervised learning bagging variance-reduces; in value-based
RL the picture is messier (\citealp{lin2024curse} shows shared-buffer
deep ensembles can underperform their own single-agent baseline). For
\emph{independent-buffer tabular} agents, the question is open. Cite
\citet{wiering2008}, \citet{osband2016bootstrapped},
\citet{lan2020maxmin}, \citet{song2023survey}.}

\guideblock{Paragraph 3 --- what is new in this paper specifically.
List the three contributions cleanly:
(a) a read-only inference rule for tabular Q-function ensembles with
heuristic-prior fallback for unseen states (Equation~1 of
Section~\ref{sec:method}), which keeps inference-time memory $O(K)$ in
trained table size and avoids the OOM failure mode of cached-update
variants;
(b) the first head-to-head comparison of soft / hard /
confidence-weighted voting in tabular Q-learning at $K$ up to 10 on a
non-toy stochastic two-player game;
(c) a compute-matched single-agent control trained on the same total
simulated-battle budget ($5{\times}10^6$ battles) as the ensemble
($10\times 5{\times}10^5$ battles), so the +12.6 pp lift cannot be
attributed to extra compute.}

\guideblock{Paragraph 4 --- result preview. State the headline numbers
plainly. $K{=}10$ ensemble: $66.8\% \pm 0.3\%$ win rate vs.\
\texttt{SimpleHeuristicsPlayer} over $100{,}000$ evaluation battles;
$+12.6$ pp over compute-matched single agent; saturating $K$ curve;
voting-rule effect $< 1$ pp at fixed $K$.}

\guideblock{Paragraph 5 --- what this paper does NOT do. Inherited
components (hierarchical action decomposition, heuristic
Q-initialization) come from our prior factorial study
\citep{singh2026v5} and are treated as fixed inputs to V6, not
contributions of this paper; full details in
Appendix~\ref{app:inherited_components}. Deep-RL ensemble extensions are
open questions and are not addressed here. Generalization across
opponents and Pokemon pools is also out of scope and is enumerated as a
limitation in Section~\ref{sec:d_limitations}.}
