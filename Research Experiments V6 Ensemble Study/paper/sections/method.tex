\section{Background and Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:prelim}

We model a Pokemon battle as a partially observable Markov decision process
(POMDP) $(\mathcal{S}, \mathcal{A}, P, r, \gamma, \Omega)$. Here $\mathcal{S}$
is the set of joint game states, $\mathcal{A}$ is the player's action set
(move 1 through 4 plus a switch), $P$ is the stochastic transition function
implemented by the Pokemon Showdown simulator, $r$ is a dense reward signal,
$\gamma$ is the discount factor, and $\Omega : \mathcal{S} \to \mathcal{O}$
is the observation function that hides opponent team information from the
agent.

Each ensemble member is a single tabular agent trained with Watkins
$Q(\lambda)$ \citep{watkins1989, sutton2018}:
\begin{align}
  \delta_t       &= r_{t+1} + \gamma \max_{a'} Q(s_{t+1}, a') - Q(s_t, a_t) \\
  e_t(s, a)      &= \begin{cases}
                     1, & (s, a) = (s_t, a_t) \\
                     \gamma \lambda \, e_{t-1}(s, a), &
                     a_t = \arg\max_{a'} Q(s_t, a') \\
                     0, & \text{otherwise}
                    \end{cases} \\
  Q(s, a)        &\leftarrow Q(s, a) + \alpha \delta_t \, e_t(s, a)
\end{align}
with replacing traces. Traces are cleared after any non-greedy exploratory
action, preserving the off-policy correctness of Watkins's algorithm.

\paragraph{Inherited components from prior work.}
Each ensemble member also uses two components developed in our prior
factorial study \citep{singh2026v5}:
\emph{(i) hierarchical action decomposition} of move-versus-switch into a
master Q-table over a 20-tuple state and a switch sub-agent Q-table over
a 17-tuple state; and \emph{(ii) heuristic Q-value initialization} that
seeds each new state-action pair with a small directional bias derived from
type effectiveness, STAB, base power, and accuracy. We summarize both
components in Appendix~\ref{app:inherited_components}; their design choices
and the factorial study that selected the winning configuration are
contained entirely in our prior work and are not contributions of this
paper. For this paper, we treat the per-member training pipeline as a
fixed black box and study what happens when $K$ such agents are combined
at evaluation time.

\subsection{The ensemble inference rule}
\label{sec:method_inference}

We now state the main methodological contribution.

\paragraph{Setup.}
At inference time we are given $K$ trained tabular agents, each holding
two Q-tables: a master $Q^k_{\text{m}}$ and a switch sub-agent
$Q^k_{\text{s}}$, for $k = 1, \ldots, K$. We want to choose an action
$a^*$ at state $s$.

\paragraph{Read-only Q-matrix construction.}
We first enumerate the legal master actions $\mathcal{A}_{\text{m}}(s)$
and build the \emph{Q-matrix}
$\mathbf{Q} \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times |\mathcal{A}_{\text{m}}|}$. Each entry
combines a member's learned Q-value with a heuristic-prior fallback for
state-action pairs the member has not observed:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{Q}_{k, a} =
  \begin{cases}
    Q^k_{\text{m}}(s, a), & \text{if } (s, a) \in \mathrm{dom}(Q^k_{\text{m}}) \\
    h(s, a),              & \text{otherwise,}
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:q_matrix}
\end{equation}
where $h$ is the deterministic heuristic prior described in
Appendix~\ref{app:inherited_components}. Crucially,
Equation~\ref{eq:q_matrix} \emph{does not write} the heuristic value back
into $Q^k_{\text{m}}$. In Appendix~\ref{app:memory_bound} we report a
practical consequence: an earlier implementation that did cache the
fallback into the dictionary grew memory at the rate of
$O(\text{unique-states-encountered})$ and was killed by the operating
system roughly $30{,}000$ battles into a $100{,}000$-battle evaluation;
the read-only formulation keeps the memory footprint constant.

\paragraph{Combination rules.}
We compare three rules for collapsing the $K$ rows of $\mathbf{Q}$ into a
single chosen action.

\emph{Soft voting} averages Q-values column-wise:
\begin{equation}
  a^*_{\text{soft}} = \arg\max_{a}
                       \frac{1}{K} \sum_{k=1}^{K} \mathbf{Q}_{k, a}.
\end{equation}

\emph{Hard voting} is plurality over per-member argmaxes, with ties broken
by mean Q:
\begin{equation}
  a^*_{\text{hard}} = \arg\max_{a}
    \Big|\big\{ k : a = \arg\max_{a'} \mathbf{Q}_{k, a'}\big\}\Big|.
\end{equation}

\emph{Confidence-weighted voting} weights each member by the spread between
its top Q-value and its row mean, then averages:
\begin{align}
  w_k &= \max\!\left(\, \max_a \mathbf{Q}_{k, a}
        - \tfrac{1}{|\mathcal{A}_{\text{m}}|} \sum_a \mathbf{Q}_{k, a},\ \ 0\right), \\
  a^*_{\text{conf}} &=
    \arg\max_{a} \frac{\sum_k w_k \mathbf{Q}_{k, a}}{\sum_k w_k}.
\end{align}
If all $w_k = 0$ we fall back to soft voting.

\paragraph{Recursion into the sub-agent.}
If the chosen master action is the switch action, we apply the same
combination rule to the sub-agent Q-tables
$\{Q^k_{\text{s}}\}_{k=1}^K$ over the available switch candidates.

\paragraph{Soundness at $K = 1$.}
For a single member the three rules collapse to the same answer: the
argmax of that member's Q-row. This is a sanity check we use in
Section~\ref{sec:r_ksat} as the leftmost point of the K-saturation curve.

\subsection{Training the ensemble}
\label{sec:method_training}

We train $K_{\max} = 30$ members independently. Each member uses
hyperparameters $\alpha = 0.1$, $\gamma = 0.99$, $\lambda = 0.7$ and a
fixed exploration rate $\varepsilon = 0.05$. These values are fixed at
the winning configuration of our prior factorial study
\citep{singh2026v5}; we do not re-tune them here. Member $k$ is seeded
with $\text{seed}_k = s_0 + k$; the only intentional source of diversity
across members is the random sequence of exploratory actions each member
draws.

All members are trained against \texttt{SimpleHeuristicsPlayer} from
\texttt{poke-env} \citep{sahovic2020pokeenv} in the
\texttt{gen4ou} format, with teams drawn from a fixed 20-Pokemon pool
through \texttt{IndexedTeambuilder}; the team given at battle index $n$
is deterministic in $n$. Each member trains for $1{,}000{,}000$ battles,
for a total of $30{,}000{,}000$ training battles. Training is
parallelized across eight \texttt{AWS EC2 c5.4xlarge} cloud instances
over fifteen days; full hyperparameter and reproducibility details are
listed in Appendix~\ref{app:reproducibility}.

\paragraph{Evaluation budget.}
At evaluation time we cap the ensemble at $K = 10$ members. This cap is
imposed by memory: each loaded member occupies approximately
$5.5$~\textsc{gib} of resident memory in our pipeline, and at $K > 10$ we
were unable to keep both the ensemble player and an opponent player
resident on a single workstation. The remaining 20 trained members are
held in reserve; the published evaluation in Section~\ref{sec:results}
uses the first 10 in seed order.

The training pipeline and the trained checkpoints are released at
\url{https://github.com/arzaansingh/poke_rl}.
