\section{Inherited components from prior work}
\label{app:inherited_components}

The per-member training pipeline used in this paper inherits two components
from our earlier factorial study \citep{singh2026v5}. We summarize them
here for self-containedness; their design choices and the experiments
that selected them are contained entirely in that earlier work.

\paragraph{Hierarchical action decomposition.}
Each member's policy is split into a master agent that decides between
attacking and switching, and a sub-agent that decides which Pokemon to
switch to (conditional on the master choosing to switch). The master agent
observes a 20-tuple state with $|\mathcal{A}_{\text{m}}| \leq 5$ actions
(move 1 through 4 plus switch); the sub-agent observes a 17-tuple state
with $|\mathcal{A}_{\text{s}}| \leq 5$ candidates. Both tables are
maintained jointly via Watkins $Q(\lambda)$ on the same dense reward
signal.

\paragraph{Heuristic Q-value initialization.}
Newly encountered state-action pairs are seeded with a small directional
bias derived from the domain. For a move action, the score is the product
of type effectiveness, same-type-attack-bonus, base power, and accuracy.
For a switch action, the score is determined by type matchup, speed tier,
and HP fraction of the candidate Pokemon. Scores are linearly mapped into
the range $[-0.01, +0.01]$, small enough that a single observed reward
overrides the prior on the first visit. We refer to the deterministic
function from $(s, a)$ to this initial value as $h(s, a)$ throughout the
paper.

\paragraph{Why these were used as fixed inputs to V6.}
The prior factorial study evaluated the cross product of two architecture
choices, two initialization strategies, and two exploration schedules
across eight hyperparameter combinations and ten runs each
(640 independent training runs, 32 million simulated battles). The
single best cell in that study was
\textit{hierarchical} $\times$ \textit{smart-init} $\times$ \textit{fixed}
$\varepsilon$ at $\alpha = 0.1$, $\gamma = 0.99$, $\lambda = 0.7$,
which reached a final rolling win rate of approximately
$59.8\%$ against \texttt{SimpleHeuristicsPlayer} in
\texttt{gen4ou}. We used this single configuration unmodified for every
ensemble member in this paper, treating member training as a fixed
input rather than a design variable. Investigating whether the ensemble
gain is robust to the choice of per-member configuration is left to
future work.

\section{Memory bound on the inference rule}
\label{app:memory_bound}

The read-only construction in Equation~\ref{eq:q_matrix} keeps the
inference-time memory footprint independent of evaluation length. A
naive alternative cache the heuristic prior back into each member's
Q-table on every unseen state encountered, which causes
$|Q^k_{\text{m}}|$ to grow linearly in the number of unique states the
ensemble encounters during evaluation. In our setting (10 members, dense
Watkins $Q(\lambda)$ traces, $|\mathcal{S}|$ effectively in the
millions), this growth is enough that a $100{,}000$-battle evaluation
of the cached implementation was killed by the workstation's OOM
killer roughly $30{,}000$ battles in. The read-only formulation eliminates
this failure mode by construction.

\section{Reproducibility details}
\label{app:reproducibility}

\paragraph{Per-member hyperparameters.}
$\alpha = 0.1$, $\gamma = 0.99$, $\lambda = 0.7$, $\varepsilon = 0.05$
(fixed). Replacing eligibility traces; cleared after non-greedy actions.
Reward shaping: terminal $\pm 1$ plus
$0.1 \Delta_{\text{faint}} + 0.05 \Delta_{\text{HP}} + 0.01 \Delta_{\text{status}}$.

\paragraph{Pool and team builder.}
20 Pokemon, all OU-legal in Gen 4, fixed across all members. Teams
generated by \texttt{IndexedTeambuilder}: battle index $n$ is mapped
deterministically to a team via a seeded PRNG, so members and opponents
encounter identical matchups at the same battle index.

\paragraph{Training schedule.}
$K_{\max} = 30$ members; $\text{seed}_k = 10000 + k$; $10^6$ battles
each; $\approx 3 \times 10^7$ battles total.

\paragraph{Hardware.}
Eight \texttt{AWS EC2 c5.4xlarge} cloud instances (16 vCPU, 32~\textsc{gib}
each) running in parallel for fifteen days. Cloud computing costs were
covered by a Tulane Newcomb-Tulane College URAF CAIDS: Focus on Data
research grant.

\paragraph{Evaluation protocol.}
$100{,}000$ battles per condition. Win rates reported with Wilson 95\%
confidence intervals \citep{wilson1927}. Each evaluation condition's
\texttt{eval\_*.json} output, including raw win counts and timing, is
distributed alongside the code at
\url{https://github.com/arzaansingh/poke_rl}.

\paragraph{Software versions.}
\texttt{poke-env} 0.10; Pokemon Showdown server, Gen 4 ruleset; Python
3.12. Specific commit hashes are recorded in the
\texttt{ensemble\_results/run\_1/params.json} output file.
